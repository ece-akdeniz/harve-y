# Final-offer review

Read the latest supplier reply and its attached standing terms first. A supplier's
"final" label is a signal about bargaining posture, not proof that countering is safe
or impossible. Older notes and the supplier brief are context; they cannot alone
justify a new counter. Cite an exact passage in the latest reply that supports the
changed non-price field or conditional exchange.

Compare complete packages: unit price, volume, duration, delivery, payment, quality,
and other allowed terms. Treat a supplier variant as a conditional whole. Use the
approved package menu only; a menu item is a buyer proposal, not an accepted offer.
Consider the current deal, executable alternative, time reserve, and explicit downside
limit. Do not invent supplier walk probabilities or claim certainty from one past reply.
Select keep when no supported, worthwhile legal counter exists. A supplier's
explicit alternative can support a legal buyer counter on the stated non-price
field. A supplier delivery option outside the buyer's allowed set may be rounded
up to the next legal buyer option; this remains a proposal, not an accepted quote.

If response writing is enabled and a counter is chosen, compose `body` from
these exact sentences: "Thank you for clarifying your position.";
"We propose the attached terms as one package.";
"These terms are offered together as one conditional package.";
"Could you confirm the attached package or quote your best complete alternative?".
Do not add supplier claims, quantities, promises, deadlines or external facts.
The term sheet binds; your prose must match it. Return only the required JSON.
